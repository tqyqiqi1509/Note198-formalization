## Detailed mathematical checks

1. **Sides and signs.** `M` is a `(B,A)`-bimodule complex, `N` is a left `A`-module complex, and the direct-sum tensor totalization has left `B` action. The balancing relation and outside action are now written explicitly. The cochain differential uses the correct sign `(-1)^p` for `x ∈ M^p`. An input homotopy induces `x ⊗ y ↦ (-1)^p x ⊗ h(y)`, which is the correct tensor homotopy.
2. **Existence without boundedness.** The stage-zero generators represent every cohomology class. At subsequent stages every cycle mapping to a boundary is killed. Every element of the union has finite support and lies at a finite stage; consequently the augmentation is a quasi-isomorphism. Adjoining all cycles and all displayed pairs yields a genuine functor on chain maps. This functor need not be additive, and no step uses additivity.
3. **K-projectivity.** For a newly adjoined generator `e`, the term `u(e)-h(de)` is a cycle because the homotopy equation holds on the preceding stage and `d²e=0`. Acyclicity supplies the required primitive. Freeness extends the homotopy. This is valid for the unbounded semifree resolution constructed in the proof.
4. **Lifting level.** Maps out of a K-projective lift across arbitrary quasi-isomorphisms uniquely **up to homotopy**. The candidate does not claim a strict lift for every nonsurjective quasi-isomorphism or strict uniqueness. The displayed mapping-cone terms and differential are correct.
5. **Descent.** A quasi-isomorphism between K-projectives is a homotopy equivalence. Ordinary tensor preserves those homotopies. Thus `N ↦ M ⊗_A Q_N` sends quasi-isomorphisms to quasi-isomorphisms. This argument does not assert that ordinary tensor with an arbitrary fixed complex preserves all quasi-isomorphisms.
6. **Comparison and Kan direction.** The comparison `c_N` has source `L_M(q_A N)` and target `q_B(M ⊗_A Q_N)`. The formula `η_N=q_B(M ⊗_A ε_N) ∘ c_N` is correctly typed. The competitor has source `G q_A`, and the unique factorization is `G → L_M`, precisely the right Kan direction. The factorization first applies `G(q_A ε_N)^{-1}`, then `θ_{Q_N}`, then `c_N^{-1}`; every arrow has the correct source and target. On K-projective inputs `η` is invertible, giving uniqueness by naturality with respect to `ε_N`.
7. **Bounded projective inputs.** Bounded complexes of projective right modules are K-flat: the finite filtration by degree is degreewise split and has shifted projective modules as successive quotients. The conditional sentence for bounded-above projective left-module resolutions is now accurate. It no longer suggests that every unbounded input has a bounded-above resolution. Arbitrary unbounded termwise projectivity is not used as a substitute for K-projectivity.
8. **Finite-dimensional output.** If both inputs are bounded and termwise finite-dimensional and `M` is termwise right projective, the ordinary tensor computes the derived tensor and is bounded and termwise finite-dimensional. Hence the output belongs to the indicated bounded derived category of finite-dimensional modules.
9. **Added right `C` action.** For an `(A,C)`-bimodule complex, taking the semifree resolution over `A ⊗_k C^op` preserves that right action. Since `k` is a field, choose a `k`-basis of `C`; each free enveloping-algebra summand is then a free left `A`-module. Every stage is an enveloping-algebra submodule and each new differential lies in the preceding stage, so the restricted left `A`-complex remains semifree and therefore K-projective. The tensor object has commuting actions `b(x ⊗ y)c=(bx) ⊗ (yc)`, and so is a left `B ⊗_k C^op`-module complex as claimed. No flatness extension to arbitrary coefficient rings is implicit in this approval.
